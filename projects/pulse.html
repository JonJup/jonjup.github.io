Research Project

PULSE — Pan-European
Freshwater Ecotypologies

A data-driven approach to freshwater river classification for Europe. PULSE changes how we evaluate typology systems and develops novel typologies that capture ecologically meaningful variation across the continent, directly informing biomonitoring and Policy implementations.

Funding DFG Walter Benjamin Fellowship
Duration 2025 – 2028
Institution University of Helsinki
PI Jonathan Jupke

About the project

PULSE compiles and harmonises pan-European biodiversity databases spanning four major taxonomic groups — diatoms, fishes, macroinvertebrates, and macrophytes — to build ecologically grounded river typologies. By combining joint species distribution models (HMSC), fuzzy clustering, and quantile random forests, the project creates classification systems that reflect real biological patterns rather than administrative boundaries.

The resulting typologies are evaluated against multiple coherence metrics and made operational through interactive prediction tools. The ultimate goal is to provide a methodological framework that member states can adopt for WFD-compliant bioassessment.

Methods

The project proceeds in three stages. First, community data across Europe are compiled, harmonised, and quality-controlled from national monitoring programmes. Second, HMSC models are fitted to estimate species environmental niches and residual co-occurrence patterns. Third, spatial fuzzy clustering (via the SKATER-CON algorithm) produces ecotypologies that are evaluated and benchmarked against biological coherence metrics.

Quantile random forest models are trained on simulated typology scenarios to predict how design decisions — number of types, variable selection, spatial extent, taxonomic resolution — affect classification quality. These predictions are served through an interactive Shiny application.

Policy relevance

PULSE directly addresses challenges in implementing the EU Water Framework Directive's typological framework. The project demonstrates how data-driven typologies can complement or improve the currently used abiotic classification systems, with concrete applications for intercalibration, reference condition setting, and ecological status assessment.
